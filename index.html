OUR PURPOSE

A diagnosis begins with a specimen.
Progress begins with a partnership.

Reliable tissue diagnosis depends on every step: collecting a skin biopsy, preparing a slide, capturing an image, and interpreting the findings.

This collaborative working group connects pathology, dermatology, and infectious diseases to strengthen that pathway through shared teaching and locally owned resources.

Virtual teaching and case discussions between UVA faculty and MUST trainees have taken place since 2025. The Fall 2026 grant proposal builds on that foundation and brings the Quito partnership into a shared educational program.

Teaching focuses on infectious and neoplastic skin disease, including leprosy, fungal infections, leishmaniasis, and skin cancer.

Fall 2026 grant proposal
Planned activities · 2027–2028
01

Strengthen the biopsy-to-slide pathway

+

Co-develop biopsy, fixation, and request-form protocols. Provide matched teaching kits at both partner sites and equip the Mbarara laboratory with imaging and diagnostic tools.

PROPOSED TARGET: ≥80% adequately completed request forms at final review.

02

Learn together through case conferences

+

Use static-image consultation for monthly teaching conferences. Log cases and review agreement with local final reports within each site as an educational quality measure.

Images support teaching and consultation; image selection and incomplete sampling remain limitations. Outcomes will be described separately for each site.

PROPOSED TARGETS: 10 conferences · ≥100 cases · ≥85% agreement in a reviewed subset · median interval below 14 days.

03

Create resources that last

+

Develop a de-identified bilingual teaching library and online atlas. Train at least two trainees per site and plan a one-month UVA rotation for one resident from each partner.

Returning residents will serve as local leads for biopsy technique and image capture. Partner institutions will own the equipment.

PROPOSED TARGET: 100 teaching cases in a shared bilingual library.
